File 3
Disease → Food Mapping

The core dataset. Maps each disease to foods to eat and foods to avoid, plus the clinical reason why. Start with 10 diseases: Type 2 Diabetes, Hypertension, High Cholesterol, GERD, Celiac, Lactose Intolerance, Gout, Hypothyroidism, CKD, PCOS.

Each entry stores dos foods, don'ts foods, food attribute tags (e.g. high_sodium, high_potassium) and the reason — used by the Gemini report to explain recommendations in plain language.

Source the rules from clinical guidelines: ADA (Diabetes), American Heart Association (Hypertension/Cholesterol), NKF KDOQI (CKD), Celiac Disease Foundation, ICMR India. Have a doctor or dietitian review before going live.

Calculators
Nutrition & Calorie Burn

Nutrition Calculator — Count-based foods multiply nutrition per unit × quantity. Bowl/cup/plate foods convert the selected size to grams first, then scale the per-100g nutrition values.

Calorie Burn Calculator — Duration workouts: MET × user weight (kg) × (minutes ÷ 60). Rep workouts: calories-per-rep × reps entered.

Rules Engine
Dos & Don'ts Engine — All Factors Considered

The engine does not only look at diseases. It considers every piece of user data together and builds a personalised recommendation from all of it.

User DataHow it influences Dos & Don'ts
Medical Conditions Primary source. Each condition maps to recommended and restricted food tags and specific foods from the disease mapping file (e.g. CKD → avoid high potassium, Diabetes → avoid high GI).
Allergies Any food matching an allergy goes directly to the don'ts list, regardless of disease rules. E.g. if the user has a nut allergy, all nut-tagged foods are blocked even if they appear in dos for another condition.
Medications Certain medications have known food interactions. E.g. Warfarin users need consistent Vitamin K intake — high Vitamin K foods are flagged with a consistency note. Diuretics flag high-sodium and potassium-rich foods. These rules are stored in the disease mapping file under medication entries.
Health Goal Changes the direction of recommendations. Lose Weight → calorie-dense foods (ghee, fried items, sweets) added to don'ts; high-fibre and low-calorie foods added to dos. Gain Weight → calorie-dense whole foods promoted. Maintain → balanced, no strong bias. Manage Condition → condition rules take full priority.
Age Adjusts what is emphasised. Older users (55+) get calcium and fibre flagged as dos. Younger users get protein-rich foods emphasised if goal is active or gain weight.
Sex Females get iron-rich foods emphasised in dos (leafy greens, lentils). Females with PCOS get additional hormonal-balance food notes. Males above 45 get prostate-relevant notes where applicable.
BMI (calculated from height + weight) Overweight (BMI > 25) → high-calorie and high-fat foods strengthened in don'ts. Underweight (BMI < 18.5) → calorie-dense whole foods added to dos. Normal BMI → no BMI-specific overrides applied.
Sleep Hours Poor sleep (< 6h) → magnesium-rich foods (nuts, seeds, leafy greens) added to dos. Caffeine-heavy items (excess tea/coffee) added to don'ts with a note about sleep disruption.
Activity Level (from workout log) High activity day → protein-rich foods promoted in dos (eggs, dal, chicken — subject to diet type + disease checks) for recovery. Sedentary day → high-calorie and high-carb foods strengthened in don'ts to avoid surplus.
Diet Type
Vegetarian / Vegan / Eggetarian / Non-veg		 Hard filter applied before all other rules. Vegan — all animal products (eggs, dairy, meat, fish) removed from dos regardless of other factors. Vegetarian — eggs and meat removed. Eggetarian — eggs allowed, meat removed. Non-veg — no dietary restriction. This is the first gate the engine passes every food through — a condition recommending chicken is irrelevant if the user is vegetarian.
Alcohol
Yes / No / Sometimes		 Regular alcohol intake → liver-supporting foods (turmeric, leafy greens, lemon water) added to dos. High-sodium snacks and processed foods strengthened in don'ts. If a condition like Fatty Liver or Hypertension is also present, the don'ts tag gets a stronger warning note.
Smoking
Yes / No / Sometimes		 Smokers have higher oxidative stress. Antioxidant-rich foods (citrus, berries, carrots, bell peppers) added to dos with a note about Vitamin C needs. Processed and fried foods strengthened in don'ts due to compounding inflammation risk.
Water Intake
Daily cups entered by user		 If below 6 cups/day — dehydrating foods (excess salt, caffeine) added to don'ts with a hydration note. If on target (8+ cups) — no specific override. Relevant for users with UTI, kidney stones, or CKD conditions where fluid intake directly affects recommendations.
Past Surgery
Type + recovery status		 Bariatric surgery → small portions only, high-protein flagged. Appendectomy (fully recovered) → no dietary restriction. Gut/bowel surgery → high-fibre foods may be flagged with a caution note. Only active or recent surgeries trigger rules; fully recovered with no ongoing impact are noted but do not change dos/don'ts.
Stress Level
1–10 scale		 High stress (7+) → magnesium-rich foods (dark chocolate, nuts, seeds), adaptogenic foods (ashwagandha, turmeric) added to dos. Sugary comfort foods added to don'ts with a note about stress-eating. Caffeine gets a moderation note as it amplifies cortisol.
Energy Level
1–10 scale		 Low energy (≤ 4) → iron-rich foods (spinach, legumes, jaggery), complex carbohydrates (oats, brown rice), and B12 sources added to dos. Simple sugars and refined carbs (white bread, maida items) added to don'ts as they cause energy crashes. Combined with low sleep and high stress, this strengthens all three sets of notes.
Overall Mood
1–10 scale		 Low mood (≤ 4) → serotonin-supporting foods (banana, oats, walnuts, dark chocolate) added to dos. Combined with high stress and low energy — forms a mental-wellbeing cluster that boosts all mood-food recommendations together.
Work / Academic Pressure
1–10 scale		 High pressure (7+) → brain-supporting foods added to dos: omega-3 rich (flaxseeds, walnuts, fish for non-veg), complex carbs for sustained focus. Junk food and high-sugar items strengthened in don'ts — they impair concentration and worsen pressure-related fatigue.
Relaxation Practices
Daily / Weekly / Occasionally / Never		 Never/Occasionally → combined with high stress and high pressure, this signals a high cortisol-risk profile. Anti-inflammatory foods (turmeric, ginger, leafy greens) get stronger promotion in dos. Processed and high-sodium snacks get a stronger don'ts note about worsening inflammation under chronic stress.

🟡 Yellow rows = data from ENERVARA Lifestyle screen. 🔴 Light red rows = data from ENERVARA Mental Wellbeing screen. White rows = data from Profile / Medical screen. All are collected in Profile Setup and stored in the users table.

Cross-Factor Collision Detection

Collisions do not only happen between two diseases. They happen between any two factors — for example, one factor recommends a food and a disease says avoid it. The engine detects all such conflicts across the full factor set before outputting any list.

Food Recommended by (factor) Restricted by (factor) Final Decision What user sees
Eggs Activity level (high protein for recovery) + Age (protein for 55+) + Goal (gain weight) Medical Condition — High Cholesterol (high dietary cholesterol restricted) Don'ts list "Eggs are good for protein and recovery but your cholesterol condition restricts dietary cholesterol. Avoid for now — consult your doctor."
Banana Medical Condition — Hypertension (high potassium recommended) Medical Condition — CKD (potassium must be restricted) Don'ts list "Banana helps with blood pressure but is restricted for kidney disease. Given both conditions, avoid it."
Nuts Sleep (poor sleep → magnesium-rich foods in dos) + Goal (gain weight) Allergy — Nut allergy Don'ts list (allergy overrides everything) "Nuts are recommended for magnesium and your weight goal, but you have a nut allergy. They are completely removed from your list."
Curd / Yogurt Medical Condition — GERD (low-acid, soothing) + Sex (female, calcium) Allergy — Lactose intolerance Don'ts list (allergy overrides) "Curd is beneficial for acid reflux and calcium, but you have lactose intolerance. Avoid dairy — try lactose-free alternatives."
Brown Rice Medical Condition — Diabetes (low GI, preferred over white rice) Medical Condition — Celiac (gluten — only if contaminated; flagged with a note) Dos list with a caution note "Brown rice is recommended for blood sugar control. Ensure it is certified gluten-free given your celiac condition."
Ghee Age (55+, traditional fat for joint health) + Goal (maintain) BMI (overweight BMI > 25 → high-fat foods restricted) + High Cholesterol Don'ts list "Ghee has traditional benefits but given your BMI and cholesterol, it is better avoided. Use in very small amounts if at all."
Coffee No factor recommends it Sleep (< 6h sleep → caffeine in don'ts) + GERD (acidic, worsens reflux) Don'ts list "Coffee is restricted because of your sleep pattern and acid reflux condition."
Spinach / Leafy Greens Sex (female, iron-rich) + Sleep (magnesium) + Goal (lose weight, low calorie) Medication — Warfarin (high Vitamin K must stay consistent, not suddenly increased) Dos list with a consistency note "Leafy greens are recommended for iron and magnesium. Since you take Warfarin, keep your intake consistent day to day — do not suddenly increase or reduce."

How the engine resolves every collision: For each food, the engine collects all factors that recommend it and all factors that restrict it. If any restriction exists, the food moves to don'ts — but the reason is always stored and shown to the user. If the restriction is a note (not an outright block), the food stays in dos with a visible caution tag.

Full priority order (highest to lowest): Allergy > Diet Type (hard filter) > Medical Condition > Medication > Past Surgery > BMI > Goal > Age / Sex > Alcohol / Smoking > Water Intake > Sleep > Stress / Energy / Mood / Pressure cluster > Activity level. A higher-priority restriction always overrides a lower-priority recommendation. Diet Type is applied first as a hard pre-filter before any other rule runs.

Phase 3 — Frontend (React + Vite)
Screen 1 — Profile Setup (5-Step Form)

Step 1 — Basic Info: Name, Date of Birth (age auto-calculated), Sex, Height (cm), Weight (kg) — BMI calculated and displayed live. State, City.

Step 2 — Lifestyle: Diet Type (Vegetarian / Vegan / Eggetarian / Non-veg — dropdown). Exercise habit (Yes / No / Sometimes). Alcohol (Yes / No / Sometimes). Smoking (Yes / No / Sometimes). Sleep hours (slider, 0–12h). Water intake (slider, cups/day).

Step 3 — Health: Medical Conditions (multi-select with search). Allergies (multi-select, with severity tag — Mild / Moderate / Severe). Medications (text input, one per line). Past Surgery (type + year + hospital + recovery status).

Step 4 — Mental Wellbeing: Overall Mood (slider 1–10). Stress Level (slider 1–10). Energy Level (slider 1–10). Work / Academic Pressure (slider 1–10). Relaxation Practices (Daily / Weekly / Occasionally / Never — toggle buttons).

Step 5 — Goal: Health goal radio — Lose Weight / Gain Weight / Maintain / Manage Condition. Completion bar shows progress across all 5 steps.

All fields stored in the users table. Every field feeds into the Dos & Don'ts engine at assessment time.

Screen 2 — Food Logger

Meal slot tabs (top bar): 🌅 Breakfast  |  ☀️ Lunch  |  🌙 Dinner  |  🍎 Snacks  |  ➕ Other. Only one tab is active at a time. Each tab is independent — adding food adds it to the currently active meal slot.

Left panel: Food categories (South Indian, Staples, Snacks, Drinks, Fruits, etc.)

Centre: Food cards — click to add. Quantity modal opens showing the appropriate picker (stepper for count-type, dropdown for bowl/cup/plate-type). Food is added to the active meal slot.

Right panel (live — per slot): Running total bar for the active slot — Calories | Protein | Carbs | Fat — updates on every add or remove. Logged foods shown below with meal-slot badge, quantity badge and ✕ remove button.

Daily Summary bar (bottom, always visible): Totals across all meal slots combined — Total Calories | Total Protein | Total Carbs | Total Fat. Updates as the user switches tabs and adds food to any slot.

Meal type is stored in food_logs.meal_type. The AI Report and Dos/Don'ts engine use the full daily log (all slots combined) for assessment. Per-slot breakdown is shown in the report for context.

Screen 3 — Workout Logger

Grid: Workout cards with icons. Click a card → drawer slides in with quantity input.

Duration workouts: Time slider (5–120 min) + preset buttons (15, 30, 45, 60 min).

Rep workouts: Number stepper (5, 10, 20, 50…).

Bottom bar (live): Total calories burned counter — updates in real time on every add or remove.

Screen 4 — Dos & Don'ts

Two-column layout: ✅ Do Eat | ❌ Avoid

Each food item shows a reason tag indicating which factor recommended or restricted it — e.g. "Hypertension", "Low Energy", "High Stress", "Diet Type (Vegan)", "Low Sleep", "BMI", "Nut Allergy". Tags are colour-coded by factor type (disease = teal, lifestyle = yellow, mental = pink, allergy = red).

⚠️ Caution items shown in a separate amber section — foods that are conditionally OK but need a note (e.g. brown rice is fine but must be gluten-free for celiac, spinach is good but keep consistent if on Warfarin).

🔴 Collision items shown in a separate orange section — food was recommended by one or more factors but blocked by a higher-priority one. Each card shows both sides: why it was recommended and why it was blocked.

Screen 5 — AI Report + Tips

"Generate My Report" button triggers the Gemini call. Loading state shows ENERVARA animation while generating.

Report card: Plain-language explanation of dos/don'ts with disease context — why each recommendation was made for this specific person.

Tips section: 3–5 personalised health improvement tips based on today's food and workout data.

Collision explanations shown in highlighted callout boxes.

Phase 4 — Gemini AI Integration

Send Gemini the user's profile, conditions, today's food and workout summary, the dos/don'ts list with reasons, and the collision list.

Gemini returns a report — plain-language explanation of why dos/don'ts were created for this person — and a tips list of practical health improvement suggestions based on today's data.

Safety rule in the prompt: Gemini explains and gives lifestyle tips only. It does not diagnose, prescribe medication, or change the dos/don'ts list.

Phase 6 — Testing
Approach
500 Dummy Users — End-to-End Validation

Generate 500 dummy user profiles with varied data to validate that the full pipeline — profile → food log → workout log → dos/don'ts → AI report — works correctly across all combinations.

What varies across the 500 users
  • Demographics: age (18–75), sex (M/F), weight (45–120kg), height (150–190cm), sleep (4–9h)
  • Conditions: all 10 diseases covered, including multi-condition combinations (e.g. Diabetes + Hypertension + CKD)
  • Goals: spread across all 4 goal types
  • Allergies: varied combinations of common allergens
  • Food logs: randomised selections across all quantity types
  • Workout logs: randomised duration and rep inputs
What we verify
  • Nutrition totals calculate correctly for all quantity types
  • Calorie burn calculates correctly for duration and rep workouts
  • Dos/Don'ts engine returns correct results for single and multi-condition users
  • Collision logic correctly flags conflicting foods and records the explanation
  • Gemini report generates without errors for all profile types
  • No user with CKD receives high-potassium foods in the Do Eat list
  • No user with Celiac receives gluten-containing foods in the Do Eat list
  • API response times are within acceptable limits under load
How we run it
  • Write a seed script that generates and inserts 500 users with randomised valid data
  • Run automated assertions on the /assessment/dos-donts endpoint for all users
  • Spot-check AI report output for 20 hand-picked edge-case profiles (e.g. 4 simultaneous conditions, all allergies set)
  • Log any cases where dos and don'ts lists overlap or collision notes are missing
